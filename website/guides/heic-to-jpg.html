How to convert HEIC and AVIF photos to JPG on Windows

Updated 5 November 2026 · Windows 10 and 11

Photos from an iPhone come as .heic, and some websites now use .avif. Windows can read both with a free extension, and OrbitDrop turns them into JPGs that open everywhere.

How to do it

  1. Get OrbitDrop, free on the Microsoft Store.
  2. Start dragging the file in File Explorer and, while you're still holding it, tap Shift. A wheel opens under your pointer. Let go on JPG. Selected 200 photos? They're all converted.
  3. If Windows is missing the free codec these photos need, OrbitDrop says which one and opens it in the Microsoft Store. Click Get, then try again.

Which extension do I need?

HEIC needs Microsoft's free HEIF Image Extensions (and the HEVC video codec, which many PCs already have). AVIF needs the free AV1 Video Extension. Windows 11 often has them already; on Windows 10 you usually need to add them once.

Copy the photos off your iPhone first

Connect the iPhone with a cable, open it in File Explorer (This PC › Apple iPhone › Internal Storage › DCIM) and copy the photos to your PC. Then convert them there.

PNG or PDF instead

The same wheel saves them as PNG, or as PDF. Several photos can also become one PDF, handy for documents you photographed.

Questions

Why can't Windows open my HEIC photos?

Windows needs a free extension from Microsoft to read HEIC. OrbitDrop tells you when it's missing and opens it for you.

Can I stop my iPhone taking HEIC photos?

Yes: on the iPhone, Settings › Camera › Formats › Most Compatible. New photos are then JPG.
