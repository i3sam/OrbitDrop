How to preview files without opening them on Windows

Updated 11 November 2026 · Windows 10 and 11

Opening a file just to check what's inside wastes time, especially when a slow app has to start first. Windows has a small preview pane built in, and with OrbitDrop you can press Space for a big preview, like on a Mac.

How to do it

  1. Get OrbitDrop from the Microsoft Store and turn on Settings › Peek › Peek with Space.
  2. Select a file in File Explorer or on your desktop and press Space. A big preview grows out of it.
  3. Press ← or → for the previous or next file, and Space or Esc to close it.

The built-in way: the preview pane

File Explorer can show a preview on the right. Press Alt + P, or choose View › Show › Preview pane (on Windows 10: the View tab › Preview pane). Click a file and its preview appears in the pane. It's narrow, and it only shows file types that have a previewer installed.

What Peek shows

Photos, videos and music play, PDFs scroll, text and code read, ZIPs show what's inside, fonts show their letters and folders their contents. More about Peek.

Questions

Why is my preview pane blank for PDFs?

The pane needs a PDF previewer. Setting Microsoft Edge or Adobe Acrobat Reader as your PDF app usually adds one.

Is Peek free?

Peek is part of the OrbitDrop upgrade ($4.99 once), and turning it on starts a free day to try it. The preview pane is free and built in.
