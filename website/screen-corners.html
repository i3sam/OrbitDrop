Features › Screen corners

Drag a file into a corner of your screen, and it's sent

Windows 10 and 11 · New in OrbitDrop 0.9

Each corner of your screen can do one thing with files: copy them to OneDrive, to a USB stick or to a folder you pick, print them, or share them. Drag a file towards a corner, it lights up and says where the file will go, and you let go.

How to use it

  1. Get OrbitDrop from the Microsoft Store.
  2. Open Settings › Screen corners and pick what each corner does. To start, the top right copies to OneDrive and the top left to a USB stick.
  3. Drag files towards a corner. It lights up and says where they'll go. Let go.

What a corner can do

  • Copy to OneDrive.
  • Copy to a USB stick: the one that's plugged in.
  • Copy or move to a folder you pick, like Projects or Invoices.
  • Print the files.
  • Share them with Windows' share window: Nearby Share, email or an app.

The corner app

Move the pointer into the bottom right corner, just above the clock, and a small Open Notepad button appears. Click it and Notepad opens. Pick any app or folder instead in Settings › Screen corners.

Never in your way

Corners only appear while you're dragging a file, and the corner app sits above the clock, away from your windows' buttons. Neither ever shows over a game, a full-screen video or the lock screen.

Questions

Does Windows 11 have hot corners?

Not like a Mac. The far right end of the taskbar can show the desktop, and that's it. See hot corners on Windows 11.

Can I move files instead of copying them?

Yes, for a folder corner: turn on Move in Settings › Screen corners.

Does it work with several monitors?

Yes. The corners are on the monitor your pointer is on.
